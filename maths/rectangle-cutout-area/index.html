Area of a Rectangle
with a Shape Cut Out

The Big Idea

When a shape is cut out of a rectangle, the area that remains is simply:

Remaining Area = Area of Rectangle − Area of Cut-out Shape

It doesn’t matter what shape is removed (circle, smaller rectangle, triangle…). As long as you can find the area of both parts, you subtract.

Visual Understanding

Imagine a large rectangle. A smaller shape is punched out of it.

The shaded region (dark) is what is left. We calculate the big rectangle’s area first, then subtract the area of the hole.

Step-by-Step Method

  1. 1. Find the area of the outer rectangle
    → length × width
  2. 2. Find the area of the cut-out shape
    → use the correct formula for that shape
  3. 3. Subtract:
    Remaining Area = Rectangle Area − Cut-out Area
  4. 4. Write the answer with the correct units (cm², m², etc.)

Common Cut-out Shapes & Formulas

Rectangle / Square

Area = length × width

Circle

Area = π × r²
(use π ≈ 3.14 or 22/7)

Triangle

Area = ½ × base × height

Semicircle

Area = ½ × π × r²

Worked Example 1 – Rectangle Cut Out

A rectangle measures 12 cm by 8 cm. A smaller rectangle of 4 cm by 3 cm is cut out.

Outer area = 12 × 8 = 96 cm²
Cut-out area = 4 × 3 = 12 cm²
Remaining = 96 − 12 = 84 cm²

Worked Example 2 – Circle Cut Out

A rectangle is 20 cm by 14 cm. A circle of radius 4 cm is cut from the middle.
Use π = 3.14

Outer area = 20 × 14 = 280 cm²
Circle area = 3.14 × 4² = 3.14 × 16 = 50.24 cm²
Remaining = 280 − 50.24 = 229.76 cm²

Key Takeaways

  • Always start with the outer rectangle area.
  • Calculate the cut-out area carefully (correct formula + units).
  • Subtract: Remaining = Outer − Cut-out.
  • Watch out for units and π approximations.
  • You can cut out more than one shape — just subtract each one.
